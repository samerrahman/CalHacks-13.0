Description of another game:
# LOCK IN

## 1. Premise and objective

You are a researcher at an isolated facility in the forest. Your team studies how an intelligent, dangerous creature responds to sound and rhythmic biological signals.

During a containment failure, the creature escapes. You flee into a small, reinforced emergency observation shelter connected to the facility’s research equipment.

The shelter has solid metal walls and no windows. You cannot see the creature directly. You experience its presence through footsteps, scraping against the walls, distant cries, interference on the security monitor, and its voice.

The creature is initially weakened by the sedative used during containment. As the sedative wears off, it becomes faster, more persistent, and increasingly capable of breaching the shelter.

**Your objective is to restore the necessary equipment, test remote lures, and complete an emergency protocol that draws the creature back into containment.**

The research now has an immediate purpose: every successful test brings you closer to surviving.

**You win** when you activate the final calibrated lure and the remote containment system successfully seals the creature inside.

**You lose** if the creature reaches the shelter and completes a breach before you can redirect it.

---

## 2. Opening and the single-room setting

The opening establishes the containment breach without requiring an outdoor playable section.

The player hears an alarm, a containment failure announcement, running through the forest, and something pursuing them. The screen remains dark until the researcher enters the shelter and slams the door.

Once inside, a damaged security recording and a short emergency message explain what happened and introduce the containment protocol.

**The only fully rendered environment is the shelter’s interior.**

The room contains:

- A terminal for tasks and research progress.
- A security monitor connected to cameras around the facility.
- A proximity detector showing the creature’s approximate distance.
- A radio or speaker for emergency messages.
- A clearly identifiable emergency equipment switch.

The external camera feeds can use prerecorded clips, changing still images, static, and occasional silhouettes. They do not require a fully rendered exterior world.

There are also **two different kinds of camera** in the experience:

- **The player’s webcam:** supplies heart-rate readings when enabled and available.
- **The fictional security cameras:** show limited information about the facility outside.

There is no glass enclosure or window through which the player watches the monster.

---

## 3. Core gameplay

The player alternates between working at the terminal and responding to the creature outside.

Tasks serve two purposes:

1. **Keep the shelter and research equipment operational.**
2. **Create misleading signals that draw the creature away.**

For the first version, the containment protocol has four stages:

1. Silence malfunctioning equipment.
2. Calibrate and test a remote heartbeat lure.
3. Repair an overloaded set of machines.
4. Calibrate a second lure that leads toward containment.

After completing these stages, the player can activate the final containment broadcast.

Research results are recorded automatically when a test succeeds. The player does not need to read long reports or manually interpret scientific data.

Each completed task produces a brief physical sound, such as a relay snapping into place or a transmitter activating. That sound gives the creature a new clue about the shelter.

However, the completed task also provides a concrete benefit: less ongoing noise, a successful distraction, or progress toward containment.

**The central decision is when to keep working, when to quiet the shelter, and when to send the creature somewhere else.**

Waiting can help during an approach, but waiting indefinitely is dangerous because the creature continues recovering from its sedation.

---

## 4. Heart rate and player physiology

Before the dangerous portion begins, the player can enable their webcam and complete a quiet calibration period.

The game establishes a personal heart-rate baseline from usable readings. Subsequent readings are compared with that baseline.

The first version uses **heart rate as its physiological input**.

Tasks can include an instruction such as:

> Silence five faults. Keep your pulse near baseline to reduce the creature’s ability to track you.

Completing the puzzle always counts. Staying near the target band makes the task safer by reducing the strength of the physiological clue the creature receives.

### How heart rate affects danger

Within the game’s fiction, the creature can sense biological rhythms at close range.

- When it is far away, machinery and remote signals dominate its search.
- When it is nearby, an elevated pulse gives it a stronger clue that someone is inside.
- A sustained elevation has more influence than one sudden reading.
- The effect has a maximum strength.
- An elevated heart rate alone never causes an automatic death.

The creature still needs to approach, investigate, and begin a breach. The player retains opportunities to respond.

If the camera is disabled or a usable reading is unavailable, the game uses neutral settings. Missing readings do not count as either extreme fear or perfect stealth.

The interface clearly shows whether heart-rate input is active.

---

## 5. Puzzle one: silence the machines

### Situation

Several malfunctioning machines inside the shelter are making noise. Their noise helps the creature locate and investigate the room.

### How it works

1. Four machine boxes appear on the terminal.
2. One or two begin shaking and glowing orange.
3. The player clicks an active box to silence that fault.
4. Another fault appears after a short interval.
5. Silencing five faults completes the task.

Clicking a quiet box causes a loud mechanical clang.

While faults remain active, they continuously generate noise. A mistaken click creates an additional, brief noise spike.

The proximity detector and its danger indication both reflect the creature’s actual approach. They should not behave like unrelated meters.

### Success

The repair ends with a brief relay sound, then the machines become quiet.

**The creature stops receiving new machinery-noise clues. It does not instantly forget where it last heard you.**

It may continue investigating the shelter before losing confidence or following another signal.

### Mistake

The clang strengthens the creature’s interest in the shelter and can cause it to approach more directly.

This is a quick visual reaction task with a clear purpose: remove an ongoing source of exposure.

---

## 6. Puzzle two: transmit a false heartbeat

### Situation

The facility has remote emitters positioned around the forest compound. They can broadcast artificial heartbeat signals.

The player uses these emitters to draw the creature away from the shelter and test which signals it follows.

### How it works

1. Three waveform cards represent three remote emitters.
2. One card flashes, identifying the emitter used for the current test.
3. Four numbered buttons display a short sequence, such as **1 → 3 → 2 → 4**.
4. The player repeats the sequence.
5. Pressing **Transmit** broadcasts the calibrated heartbeat from the selected remote emitter.

The three cards identify the transmission destination. They do not introduce a separate guessing puzzle.

### Heart-rate effect

- **Near baseline:** the sequence plays at its normal tempo.
- **Elevated pulse:** playback becomes modestly faster, with more unsettling audio and visual presentation.
- **No usable reading:** playback uses the normal tempo.

The sequence always contains four inputs. Playback speed has a fixed upper limit, and the player has a generous response window.

The tempo is set when the sequence begins, so it does not unpredictably change halfway through.

### Success

The local console makes a brief activation sound, but the remote emitter produces a stronger, sustained signal elsewhere.

**A successful transmission reliably draws the creature toward the remote emitter for a short period.**

This creates an opportunity to work and records another result for the containment protocol.

### Mistake

The console emits an error sound, and the sequence restarts. The sound can draw the creature closer, but previously completed research stages remain complete.

This is a four-button memory task with a clear purpose: create a convincing false lead.

---

## 7. Monster behavior and AI

The creature reacts to a consistent set of information:

- Active machinery noise.
- Mistake and completion sounds.
- Remote heartbeat transmissions.
- The player’s recent pulse relative to baseline, when available.
- Its own position and what it has recently investigated.
- Its current stage of recovery from sedation.

It does not automatically know everything happening inside the shelter.

Silencing equipment removes new evidence. A decoy gives it a competing lead. Its memory of previous sounds influences where it searches next.

### What AI controls

AI chooses among valid tactics, such as:

- Investigating a recent sound.
- Circling the shelter.
- Stopping nearby to listen.
- Following a remote signal.
- Waiting before approaching again.
- Delivering a short, context-dependent taunt.

The AI must affect the creature’s behavior as well as its dialogue.

For example, after the player silences the machines while their pulse remains elevated, the creature might choose to stop and listen near the wall, then whisper:

> “You made it quiet. I can still hear you.”

### What the game rules control

Movement speed, sensing range, successful lure effects, attack warnings, and breach conditions follow consistent rules.

An AI response cannot teleport the creature, cancel an earned puzzle reward, or kill the player without the required approach and warning.

The proximity detector, footsteps, camera events, and voice effects all use the same underlying creature position.

As sedation wears off, the creature searches faster and gives the player less time to respond. That escalation creates urgency without requiring the player to watch a separate countdown.

---

## 8. Blackouts

Occasional electrical failures shut down the shelter’s main lights and displays.

During a blackout:

- The distance display disappears.
- The danger meter disappears.
- The security feed goes offline.
- Visual puzzles pause, preserving their progress.
- The creature continues moving outside.

The player must use sound to decide when it is safe to restart the equipment.

### How the player survives

A familiar emergency switch remains usable by a fixed keyboard control or a faintly illuminated physical button.

The player can shut down the remaining noisy equipment, then listen.

Approaching footsteps, breathing near the wall, and scraping indicate that restarting would be dangerous. Clearly receding footsteps indicate an opportunity to restore power.

Restarting the equipment produces a noticeable startup sound. Doing it while the creature is still close can bring it back toward the shelter.

Keeping everything shut down prevents task progress while the creature continues recovering. The player therefore has a reason to listen carefully and resume work when an opening appears.

Blackouts occur at eligible moments with a cooldown between them. They do not interrupt an already unavoidable lethal attack.

**The blackout is a survival decision based on sound.**

---

## 9. Atmosphere, voice, and jumpscares

Most of the horror comes from the creature’s unseen presence and the player’s uncertainty about how close it is.

As it approaches:

- Distant movement becomes distinct footsteps.
- Footsteps become breathing, scraping, and contact with the walls.
- Its voice becomes clearer and more immediate.
- The room’s metal surfaces begin to vibrate or dent.

Jumpscares can include a sudden impact against the wall, a security-feed interruption, a silhouette on a monitor, or a burst of movement immediately outside.

Some scares can be nonlethal atmospheric events. A real breach attempt has a consistent warning cue that the player can learn.

The creature speaks occasionally, responding to meaningful events such as a mistake, a successful repair, or a sustained pulse elevation. It does not continuously narrate the game.

If a generated line is unavailable, prepared dialogue keeps the experience running.

On death, a final impact, deformation of the door, and a cut to black can communicate the breach without requiring a full monster reveal.

---

## 10. End-of-game heart-rate report

After victory or death, the game presents a short report connecting the player’s recorded pulse with events during the run.

It includes:

- The calibration baseline.
- The highest usable recorded heart rate.
- A heart-rate timeline with markers for scares, blackouts, mistakes, and close encounters.
- The event followed by the largest recorded increase.
- Recovery after major events, when enough readings are available.

Use wording such as:

> “Your largest recorded pulse increase followed the second blackout.”

or:

> “Your pulse rose most during the encounter at the door.”

The report describes measured responses associated with events. It does not assign a definitive fear score.

If heart-rate input was unavailable, the end screen shows ordinary gameplay results and marks physiological statistics as unrecorded.
