# Track fit and judging plan

The strongest submission is a complete, readable horror game: an unseen creature follows sound evidence; quality-gated pulse supplies a small close-range clue; the player can always interrupt with quiet, redirect it with an earned lure, and finish a fair containment protocol. Gemini selects legal behavior, ElevenLabs supplies contextual speech, and Convex makes the decision history visible to a consenting observer. Do not turn the shelter into a collection of unrelated sponsor features.

## A two-minute demo that proves the game

Use a known ordinary seed and the normal rules for a two-minute continuous slice, not hidden state manipulation or an unimplemented demo preset. Rehearse the full flow before judging and retain a separately completed full win/loss for verification. If showing real sensing, complete permission, warmup and 30 usable calibration seconds **before** the two-minute pitch; never accelerate the sensor clock, swap in a synthetic trace or promise that a real person's pulse will elevate on demand. If calibration fails, choose manual, keep the UNAVAILABLE label and demonstrate the game honestly.

| Time | Player-visible action | What the judge learns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:15 | Dark opening: alarm, flight, metal door slam. Show the solid shelter and a single arrow pointing to the terminal | The world is conveyed by sound; the webcam and fictional CCTV are distinct; this is a containment-research loop |
| 0:15–0:35 | Follow the brief numbered-key tutorial and silence active faults; show one intentional mistaken quiet-box click only if the current warning window permits it | Noise is evidence, proximity and footsteps share a real position, and task progress has a concrete benefit |
| 0:35–0:55 | Watch the highlighted emitter and four-button sequence, replay if needed, then transmit | The successful remote signal overrides weaker local clues and earns a reliable 16-second diversion |
| 0:55–1:15 | Work on the next repair. At an eligible blackout or approach, press Q, hear listening then receding movement, and hold F to restore | Escape works without a model, pulse or vision; blackout pauses visual puzzles but not the creature. Do not force a blackout in an ineligible/lethal state just to fit the script |
| 1:15–1:40 | Continue the next available research step and point to the accepted director decision on the separate observer | Completed stages are preserved, sedation creates urgency, and earned rewards cannot be canceled by AI |
| 1:40–2:00 | Show the current progress and source/quality labels; let the observer receive the actual latest event | This is a continuous slice, not a claimed full win. Explain the remaining lure/final broadcast and offer the full playable run |

After a complete run, demonstrate the real result and, only with the player's report consent, its local physiological timeline. This is a separate proof from the short pitch; do not invent a victorious endpoint or pulse graph to fit two minutes. A short prerecorded capture may supplement a flaky venue network only when labeled RECORDED with its timestamp/model and the live app's current fallback shown. A recorded pulse trace is never a LIVE source.

For the Gemini moment, point to a legal decision such as LISTEN_NEAR after machines go quiet and the corresponding footsteps pause outside the wall. Explain its event IDs in one sentence. Then use Q and show that the local quiet reward wins over any pending model reply. For ElevenLabs, let the line finish and display the exact caption; do not drown it under narration. For Convex, let the second client update visibly and demonstrate delete after the pitch. No physiological graph is sent to that second client.
