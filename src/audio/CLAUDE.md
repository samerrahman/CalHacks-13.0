# /src/audio — Lea (engine), Lucy (speech queue)

HUSH's Web Audio engine handles the 44 bundled effects (27 original syntheses, 17 derived from CC0 recordings), positional sources, power-dependent beds, radio processing, bounded volume/mute and optional speech playback. `queue.ts` owns speech identity, expiry, priority, invalidation and cancellation. Neither module owns gameplay rules or provider requests.

- The bundled WAVs are original mono PCM effects, not human recordings or provider-generated voices. Keep their filenames and source metadata aligned with `public/assets/audio/audio-manifest.json` and the runtime asset manifest.
- One authoritative creature position drives footsteps, scraping, breathing, creature voice and CCTV. `updateWorld` reads that vector and `lastMotion`; never create a separate threat clock or random audio location. Stop an approach/recede cue when movement direction changes. Distant listening must not emit a close-wall breath. `setListenerPosition` and `setListenerYaw` should follow the actual render-camera pose when moving into equipment inspection.
- Place persistent lure audio at the actual remote emitter. Room hum and active-machine beds follow equipment/pause state. Use short gain transitions for loops; never loop an impact, warning or intro accidentally.
- All output uses the bounded master gain and compressor. Mute, pause, reset, reduced-impact preferences and listener orientation must remain functional. Start audio only after a player gesture; loading failure must not hide captions or block input.
- There is no alarm siren. `updateWorld` drives the creature soundscape from `creature.distance` (0–24), its heard `position` and `hunting`: footsteps with an explicit distance gain curve (direction-only panners, rolloff 0), faster when closer and a run during a hunt; occasional wall pounding within two steps and a barrage while `phase === 'breach'`; growls within five; crickets that fade out as it nears; a wind bed; and random creaks, groans, snaps and distant howls. All of it stops on pause, reset and disposal. Polyphony is capped at 16 one-shots.
- `playScare('impact'|'monitor'|'breach', position?, soft?)` combines an existing authored mechanical hit with a short synthesized downward tone, plus a synthesized horror layer: impact/monitor add a short positional noise burst and dissonant stab; breach adds a 3.4-second non-positional scream (three detuned sawtooth voices gliding 380→980→1080→620 Hz with vibrato and jitter through open-vowel formants, plus rasp noise and an octave-down growl, distorted). It runs on its own scare bus through a gentle clipper into the limiter at `min(0.85, volume × 1.7)` (0 when muted), with the recorded CC0 scream layered twice and a growl as the face holds and opens the limiter to -6 dB, so it is louder than any other sound but no longer painfully so (lowered 2026-10-25 at the user's request, with quieter impact hits and stings). Calls are bounded to one per 0.65 seconds, stay under master volume/mute and the compressor, and stop every layer (restoring the limiter) on pause/reset/disposal. Softer scares keep the gentler gain/attack and never open the limiter.
- Every generated speech request carries its exact caption and latest-start expiry. Active speech is never interrupted by a higher numeric priority. Cancel obsolete requests after pause/task/run changes; alarms and scare effects remain independent of the speech slot. Live/cache metadata must match the actual source. Missing audio falls back to a readable caption hold and a chime.
- Creature speech uses a stronger local Web Audio treatment: slowed playback, low-pass body, driven waveshaper growl and a short filtered double. Human radio speech retains its separate radio filter. Keep the same source attached to the creature position and bounded by the master gain, mute, pause and queue cancellation.
- `subscribeSpeech` reports actual started/ended/cancelled/idle events. Captions are shown only on started, including caption-only fallback; provider completion alone does not display them. `isSpeechBusy` includes decoding and fallback reading time. `src/ui/voiceScheduler.ts` reserves authored requests before provider work and prevents a faster creature response from skipping the opening instruction.
- Provider credentials remain outside renderer/audio code. Audio receives a validated playback result from the integration layer; never fetch provider APIs directly here or claim the bundled effects are ElevenLabs output.
- Dispose sources, nodes, pending fetches and AudioContext. Reset must stop old beds, attached speech and effects before a new run.
- Audio tests cover queue behavior and source placement using a mocked Web Audio graph. `npm run assets:validate` checks actual WAV format, peak and loop boundaries. Neither proves physical speaker loudness or a live provider result.
- Fault sounds are four procedural, whole-cycle 1.2 s loops built at start (thump, whine, ticking, buzz). `AudioWorld.faultSounds` maps machines to sounds and each failing machine loops its sound from its rack slot; `AudioWorld.listening` plays the rack key one machine at a time.
